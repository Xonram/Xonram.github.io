I spent the last three days in Montpellier, attending a [GALS](https://gals.pages.math.cnrs.fr/) conference. It is the first time I got to meet people working in the same field as I am. We had a hotel booked for two days, and everything was paid for by the university.

There were two courses given, one on locally grossly bounded groups, from which I did not understand much, as I have very little knowledge in geometric group theory. From this course, I understood that what we want to do is to transfer our tools from the geometric theory of locally compact groups to groups that aren't locally compact. For this, we define locally grossly bounded groups, which are groups in which whatever metric we use, there are always neighbourhoods of finite diameter. Such groups include for example the mapping class group of surfaces of infinite type. What I understood is that most of what we know on locally compact groups still hold in this case as well.

The second course was on billiards and geodesic flows on surfaces. This is closer to what I do, so I had an easier time understanding. Take a billiard, and each time the ball hits an obstacle, create a copy of the billiard. Then, create a [wormhole-like](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wormhole#/media/File:Einstein-rosen-bridge-model.png) link between the two copies of the obstacle. The geodesic flow 'converges', when the space between the two copies goes to zero, to the flow on the billiard. Thus, you can prove things on the billiard flow while working on the above surface. The rest of the course talked about the tools that help us go from one flow to the other.

On Thursday evening, we all went out for dinner. It still angers me how it's normal for everyone to just eat animal products in theses spaces. I maybe got a little too used to Rennes, which was a place in which I didn't eat often with people who ate meat. But, when I got in Nantes, it wasn't a question people were even asking themselves. Moreover, you cannot go out of your way as a PhD. student to denounce these practices, because, on top of being counterproductive, I don't think it's understandable, coming from a professor, to listen to some young student who just started two weeks ago explain to you how you should live your life, but it still angers me nonetheless.

I went back to the hotel by foot, and talked with some professor for a small hour walking back through Montpellier's city center. It was very nice, I enjoyed our discussion, but it still felt weird being treated as an equal by people 20/30 years older than me.

Overall, I think I like this format of conferences. Enough mathematical labour so that it feels useful, but plenty of time to talk about other things with people that were there. I mostly talked to other PhD students, and I also got to meet a person I had met on the internet, and she was very nice. For now, I look forward to going to these kind of conferences more.
